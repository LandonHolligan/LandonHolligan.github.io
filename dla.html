Electrostatic Tractor Supercharging

Discovery Learning Apprenticeship · CU Boulder

Through CU Boulder’s Discovery Learning Apprenticeship, I used SPIS, SIMION, and MATLAB to study electrostatic tractor supercharging for space debris removal. My simulations showed a 71.2% increase in calculated electrostatic force through supercharging.

The research

I investigated how placing a target spacecraft in a plasma wake could improve electrostatic tractor performance. The goal was to understand when supercharging could occur and how it could help with contactless space debris removal.

Modeling wakes and electron beams

I used SPIS to characterize plasma wakes and study how spacecraft potentials affected the wake and ion collection. In SIMION and MATLAB, I studied beam spread, target impacts, and electron recollection to find a useful balance between getting electrons onto the target and limiting their return to the servicer.

What I found

My validation simulation showed a 71.2% increase in calculated electrostatic force through supercharging. In a separate application case, the model retained 96.3% of the initial force after reducing electron-beam energy from 15 keV to 5 keV. That result showed how the wake could help sustain charging with less beam power.

Research team

I completed this work in CU Boulder’s AVS Laboratory through the Discovery Learning Apprenticeship, working with graduate mentor Kaylee Champion and faculty advisor Hanspeter Schaub.
