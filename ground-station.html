2-Axis Ground Station & 5.8 GHz Dish

Primary designer · CU Sounding Rocket Laboratory

As the primary mechanical engineer, I designed and built SRL’s two-axis ground station and 5.8 GHz dish for live telemetry and video downlink. I carried the system from mechanical design and tolerance analysis through fabrication, assembly, and testing, achieving approximately 0.2° average positioning error across multiple commanded-versus-measured tests.

Mechanical design

I designed nearly the whole mechanical system, from the azimuth and elevation structures to the drivetrain, main stanchion, counterweights, dish attachment, and antenna fixture. A big part of the work was making those components fit together and building something we could actually manufacture and assemble.

Building the hardware

I personally machined the baseplate, table structure, and main stanchion. We sent the aluminum sheet-metal dish mount and azimuth mounting plate out for waterjet cutting, and had the counterweights machined at the shop. I carried the design through fabrication and assembly with the team.

Dish and antenna

I designed the 5.8 GHz dish and its antenna fixture as part of the ground station. The dish was 3D printed in PETG, with a 2 mm surface-profile requirement guiding the design.

Testing and integration

We compared commanded angles with physically measured angles across several tests and measured approximately 0.2° average positioning error. I also lead four members developing harnessing solutions and PCB fixtures for the elevation section, helping them work through CAD and mechanical design decisions.
